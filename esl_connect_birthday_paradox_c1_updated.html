Why Is Our Intuition So Wrong?

This C1 lesson explores the famous birthday problem and why a relatively small group can produce a surprisingly high probability of two people sharing a birthday.

  4. Do you trust intuition when dealing with numbers? Why or why not?
  5. How can reframing a problem make it easier to solve?
  6. Why might non-linear growth be difficult to understand intuitively?

C1 Grammar: Inversion and Cleft Structures

Use advanced emphasis to explain surprising mathematical ideas.

StructureFunctionExample
Only when / only after + inversionemphasises the point at which something becomes clearOnly when we count the pairs do we see why the probability is so high.
Not only + inversion..., but...adds a second important pointNot only does the number of pairs increase, but it increases quadratically.
What + clause + be...focuses attention on the key causeWhat makes the result surprising is the speed at which combinations grow.
It is/was + focus + that...highlights a particular factorIt is the number of possible pairs that drives the probability upward.
Had + subject + past participle...formal third conditional inversionHad we relied only on intuition, we might have guessed a much larger group.

Grammar Exercise

1. Rewrite with Only when...: We count the pairs. Then the result makes sense.
2. Rewrite with Not only...: The number of pairs increases. It increases quadratically.
3. Rewrite with What...: The rapid growth in combinations makes the result surprising.
4. Rewrite with It is...: The number of possible pairs drives the probability upward.
5. Rewrite using inverted third conditional: If we had relied only on intuition, we might have guessed a much larger group.
6. Rewrite with Only after...: We calculate the probability of no match. Then we can find the probability of a match.
7. Rewrite with What...: The large number of possible pairs explains the high probability.
8. Rewrite with Not only...: The birthday problem challenges intuition. It also shows how easily coincidences can be misunderstood.

Role Play: Explain the Birthday Problem

Explain a counterintuitive idea clearly to someone who does not trust the result.

Situation: One student is convinced that 23 people cannot possibly produce a greater-than-50% chance of a shared birthday. The other has to explain the logic without relying on complex formulas.

Student A: The Skeptic

  • Say why 23 seems far too low.
  • Question the assumptions.
  • Ask why 253 pairs matter.
  • Challenge the idea that coincidences can be common.

Student B: The Explainer

  • Explain why the problem is flipped.
  • Explain complementary probability.
  • Explain why the number of pairs grows quickly.
  • Use at least two inversion or cleft structures.

Final task: Student A decides whether the explanation has changed their intuition.
